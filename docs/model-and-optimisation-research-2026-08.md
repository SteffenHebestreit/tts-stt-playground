# German-First TTS/STT Platform — 2026 Model & Architecture Decision Document

*For the maintainer of `e:/tts-stt`. Every factual claim is linked to a primary source. Anything without one is marked **(unverified)**.*

---

## 1. Bottom line

Your roster is close to the frontier for German — but you are running **generic checkpoints where German-dedicated ones exist under the same licence, same runtime, same VRAM**. Three near-one-line changes capture most of the available model-side gain: German Whisper turbo in `stt-service`, `parakeet-primeline` in `parakeet-asr-service`, and `t3_model='v3'` in `chatterbox-tts-service`.

Beyond that, **the models are fine and the architecture is the bottleneck**. The live path re-decodes an 8 s window roughly twice a second; its LocalAgreement prefix comparison is indexed against a *sliding* buffer and therefore collapses as soon as the window rolls; there is no endpointer, so `final` means "the session ended" and can mean decoding up to `WS_MAX_BUFFER_S=600` seconds in one pass; and every model in the stack stays resident in VRAM forever, with `piper-training-service` holding a GPU reservation inside the default `all` profile.

Exactly one genuinely new *capability* is worth buying: a true-streaming German ASR to retire the window loop. You may already own it — Qwen3-ASR has an [official incremental streaming API](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-ASR/blob/main/examples/example_qwen3_asr_vllm_streaming.py) you are not using. [Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime-2602) is the better answer if you have 24 GB. On TTS, **nothing beats what you have installed for German quality**; the only thing worth adding is [MOSS-TTS-Realtime](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-TTS-Realtime)'s streaming session API, which would let you delete the sentence-chunking layer entirely.

### Does a true-streaming German ASR exist that would let you stop re-decoding a sliding window?

**Yes — three of them, and one is already in your stack.**

The architecturally purest is **Nemotron 3.5 ASR streaming**: NVIDIA's docs state it ["processes only each new incoming chunk while reusing cached encoder context from prior chunks"](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/main/en/model_doc/nemotron3_5_asr), against your current loop's ~16× re-encode of every frame. Algorithmic latency is a runtime switch (80/160/320/560/1120 ms). But its German is the weakest of anything recommended here, and there is a live, NVIDIA-acknowledged complaint — [discussion #11, "Model Underperformance with German Language"](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/nemotron-3.5-asr-streaming-0.6b/discussions/11), with confirmed failures on compound nouns (*Bahnhofplatz→Barnusplatz*, *Herzkranzgefäße→Herzgrenzefäße*). NVIDIA's response was three workarounds (word boosting, German KenLM fusion, or [fine-tuning](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/fine-tuning-nemotron-35-asr)), not a fix. Its headline benefit — 240 concurrent streams on an H100 — is worth nothing to one German-speaking user.

**Voxtral-Mini-4B-Realtime is the better German streaming choice**, and the only model in this entire survey that combines purpose-built streaming, Apache-2.0, *and* a published per-language German WER at every latency setting. 4.87 % FLEURS German at 960 ms delay is offline-Whisper-class, so switching the live path would not regress German quality. Two caveats you must close yourself: the realtime path documents no language-forcing parameter (German appears to rely on auto-detection — test on your own audio), and it means adding a vLLM container that will not comfortably share 12 GB with your TTS services.

**Before either, spend an afternoon on Qwen3-ASR.** You are running the weights already. Moving `qwen3-asr-service` to the vLLM backend and exposing the streaming session API gives you true incremental decoding with zero new weights, no new licence and no new GPU budget. Two restrictions are verbatim on the card: ["Streaming inference does not support batch inference or returning timestamps"](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-ASR-1.7B) — so streaming mode cannot serve your `piper-training` segmentation pipeline, which needs timestamps — and streaming costs ~0.6–0.9 WER over offline on the card's own inference-mode table. The blocker is that **no German WER is published for it at all**, so this is a "measure it yourself" decision, not a "read the card" one.

For the **batch/file** path, do the two checkpoint swaps and stop. `primeline/whisper-large-v3-turbo-german` posts the best "All" score (2.628 %) in [its own comparison table](https://huggingface.co/primeline/whisper-large-v3-turbo-german) while being smaller and faster than large-v3 (809M, 4 decoder layers vs 32). `parakeet-primeline` is the throughput answer at [RTFx 3332.74 for the base architecture](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3) — note it is **not** the accuracy leader; on the same benchmark family the Whisper German turbo beats it 2.628 vs 2.95.

### What actually beats what you have

**Nothing beats Chatterbox v3 on German quality.** Its `<0.20 % CER` is vendor-reported with no stated test set, but it is *the only German-specific published TTS quality number in this entire survey*. Every streaming challenger — MOSS-Realtime, CosyVoice3, Pocket TTS, MOSS-Nano — publishes **no German quality metric of any kind**. So the trade is explicit: you would be swapping a measured German quality number for a measured latency number. Do not make that swap without an A/B on your own German text.

**Time-to-first-audio is where the real gap is, and it is architectural, not a model deficiency.** Chatterbox has no streaming API in the open-source repo — [the HF card documents `generate()` only](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox) — which is precisely why `chatterbox-tts-service` implements sentence-chunking. MOSS-TTS-Realtime is the one candidate that removes that layer rather than optimising it: a documented incremental `push_text()`/`end_text()` session with KV-cache reuse across turns, at **TTFB 180 ms / RTF 0.51 on a single NVIDIA L20** — [the only latency figure in this survey that names its hardware](https://github.com/OpenMOSS/MOSS-TTS/blob/main/docs/moss_tts_realtime_model_card.md), and on weaker silicon than Chatterbox's H100-measured 300 ms.

Two flags before you touch either. Chatterbox applies [PerTh watermarking to *every* generated file](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox), self-hosted included — if your users expect unwatermarked audio, v3 does not change that but you should know it. And for `piper-tts`: both CPU challengers (Pocket TTS `german_24l`, MOSS-Nano) offer 48 kHz output, cloning and streaming at Piper's deployment cost, but **neither publishes a single German quality or German speed number**. At 100M params the benchmark costs an afternoon; do that before writing a service.

> **A correction that saves you effort:** the in-code comment implying a smaller `WS_WINDOW_S` cuts cost ~3× is wrong. faster-whisper always pads the mel to 3000 frames before the encoder ([`audio.py:111`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/master/faster_whisper/audio.py), [`transcribe.py:1180`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/master/faster_whisper/transcribe.py)), and large-v3-turbo keeps large-v3's full 32-layer encoder (only decoder layers drop 32→4). An 8 s and a 25 s window cost the **same** encoder pass. Raising `WS_WINDOW_S` back up is nearly free and buys more left context. That sets the floor for everything below.
